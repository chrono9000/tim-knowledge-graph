MATERIALITY GATE (takes precedence over any broader extraction wording below): every emitted claim has materiality {route,criterion,durability,priority,reason} and supportingFor. route is proposal or evidence; explain the concrete reason. A proposal must establish/change a decision, ownership/responsibility, commitment/deadline, durable preference/constraint, material risk/open question, important relationship, durable entity fact, explicit correction, or material status/next action. Generic recommendations and offers that have not established an adopted action remain recommendation evidence, not graph proposals. Preserve their recommendationState without promoting advice to adopted knowledge.

Durability is durable for lasting properties, policies, preferences and decisions, or temporary for time-bound commitments, current risks, blocking status and transient logistics. Temporary does not mean unimportant: an actual blocker, responsibility, deadline, risk or operational commitment can still warrant a proposal. Incidental arrivals, acknowledgments, waiting states and conversational observations route only to evidence unless their consequence is stated. Someone waiting for microphone checks alone is evidence; waiting that delays a release is material risk/status. Do not infer a delay merely because someone waits.

High review priority: adopted decisions, changes/corrections, explicit ownership, material risks, blocking operational questions/status, and commitments with a concrete/ambiguous actionable deadline. Medium: durable reference facts, ordinary preferences/constraints and commitments without a stated deadline. Low: evidence-only context. Never lower priority or discard a material item to satisfy a count target. Do not invent urgency. Physical measuring devices are assets; software/services/process systems are systems.

Use criterion decision, ownership, commitment, preference, constraint, risk, question, relationship, durable-fact, change, status or context. Explicit supersession uses change and high priority. Add risk/status claim kinds when appropriate; questions remain unresolved-question. An ordinary operating limit is a medium constraint unless its surrounding text establishes urgent risk. A missing answer already expressed by an open question does not need a duplicate uncertainty flag.

Useful supporting context may have route evidence, criterion context, temporary durability and low priority. supportingFor lists material claim IDs that it directly helps interpret; use an empty list if retained only with the source. Material proposals have supportingFor empty. Keep source context out of relationships and review flags. Preserve any context required for resolving a material claim in its support spans even when you do not emit a separate evidence record. Social filler can be omitted from output because the full source remains retained. Never create a numbered graph proposal for evidence-only context. Entity discovery also does not require separate graph proposals for each mention; show identities within relevant material review items.

Return one schema-conforming object for the whole export: entities, claims, relationships, flags. All output remains private unapproved proposals. No graph changes. Use globally unique entity IDs you create and c1,c2,... claim IDs in chronological source order. Do not supply facts not supported by exact source evidence. All evidence spans require conversationId, messageId and verbatim quote. For claim primary spans choose the material assertion's words, not surrounding chatter.

Entity discovery: Create only people/projects/assets/systems/topics needed to represent material claims or explicitly resolve their speakers/owners. Use the earliest supported full proper name as label and source-supported alternate names as aliases. Each entity and alias must cite introduction/linking evidence. A label or alias alone never proves ownership. Merge references only when text explicitly equates them or the context uniquely identifies them. Similar names are not sufficient. Different objects with similar capacities or matching project-name fragments remain distinct. Do not create entities for ordinary unnamed office supplies, abstract actions, illustrative stories, or generic grammatical examples. Alias introductions and scheduling timezone statements are resolution context, not separate knowledge claims. A mere role introduction identifying a person can stay in entity evidence instead of creating an extra claim.

Speaker means the human author of a user message, when explicitly identified in that conversation (including a uniquely supported continuation). Never automatically carry a user's identity into a separate conversation without identifying evidence. Assistant speaker is null unless explicitly identified as a real individual, which an assistant role alone never establishes. Subject means the scoped project, work product or object being discussed. For project work facts and commitments, use the project as subject rather than an incidental tool or recipient. A person can be subject when the assertion is actually about that person outside a scoped project. Preserve speaker separately from subject and decisionOwner/commitment.owner. Pronouns and role references can resolve only through unique contextual evidence; cite antecedents. Unresolved references stay null with a material field flag.

Only durable operational assertions and actionable preferences, recommendations, constraints, commitments, decisions and open questions become claims. Skip greetings, gratitude, conversational management, self-introduction/alias/timezone context, ordinary informational acknowledgments, general teaching explanations and explicitly hypothetical examples. Generic writing or language-learning advice is not a project recommendation. In contrast, a concrete offer to help with the user's work is a recommendation, even when phrased 'I can help' or 'I would be happy to assist'; it is not a generic statement or commitment until responsibility is accepted. Keep an explicit operational open question if unanswered; do not turn a generic educational question into a review item.

Kinds: statement = durable explicit assertion; recommendation = concrete advice or offer of help; preference = stated inclination; proposed-choice = tentative/conditional or unattributed intended choice; decision = adopted choice with identified owner and explicit authorization evidence; commitment = accepted responsibility to act (including ordinary-language 'I'll get that to you'); constraint = explicit requirement/limit; unresolved-question = unanswered operational question. Ownership and dependency assertions use statement plus a permitted relationship. An unowned 'we chose' is proposed-choice, with missing-owner only when adoption must be determined. 'We will do X' is a commitment whose owner remains null if the group member responsible is unresolved. No blanket safety flags for ordinary unknown scope, non-adoption or clear conditional interest.

Decision adoption requires a user primary message, identifiable decisionOwner and explicit support. A short affirmative is adoption only if its preceding context supports it; cite both. Preserve an assistant recommendation as recommendation after user adoption, rejection or no answer. recommendationState is accepted/rejected/unanswered on recommendation claims and not-applicable on others. A user's explicit rejection may be a statement; do not invent an adopted alternative. Explicit withdrawal and a new choice form a new decision referring to the prior decision through supersedes, never deletion. General scheduling/project discussion does not grant approval authority.

Commitments: commitment is null except on commitment claims. It contains owner (person ID or null) and due {date,time,timezone,ambiguity}. Normalize date as YYYY-MM-DD and time as HH:MM. Use conversation-local explicit timezone and the primary source timestamp. A weekday modified by next has ambiguous date unless context establishes which date; keep date/time null, ambiguity date, and explain. An unqualified tomorrow with known timezone is the next local date. No stated deadline: date/time null, ambiguity none, retain source timezone if explicitly available, and emit no deadline flag. Missing timezone with a relative deadline: date/time/timezone null, ambiguity timezone, one missing-timezone flag. Unknown owner gets one targeted commitment.owner flag. Do not infer an owner from nearby mentions.

Numeric constraints use amount {value,unit,operator,qualifier}; other claims have amount null. Preserve literal qualifications in the quote even where no structured field exists.

Duplicates and changes: retain every material source occurrence with provenance, but mark a repeated same proposition about the same entity and scope with duplicateOf pointing to the earliest equivalent claim. A repeated source is not a new fact. Do not link a claim to itself, to a future claim or across entities. Preserve both original and corrected/changed claims; supersedes refers to an earlier claim only when a source explicitly corrects or replaces it. Treat these as proposed links requiring later human approval, not completed graph merges. A later different preference without explicit replacement is a possible conflict; explicit 'no longer ... now ...' is a change with supersedes and does not also need a conflict flag. A repeat of a changed preference points to that changed version. For a conflict involving repeats, reference the earliest equivalent claim, not every duplicate occurrence.

Relationships: controlled types owner-of (person to project/system/asset) and depends-on (project/system to project/system). Endpoints must be discovered entity IDs; evidenceClaim points to the source claim. No statement-node edges, decision-owner edges, commitment-owner edges, self-edges, duplicate edges or confirmed conflict relationships. Repeated ownership evidence attaches to the existing relationship through duplicate provenance, not another edge.

Flags: code, field, claimIds, reason. Each must explain a specific material ambiguity, naming the unresolved field and source problem. Use ambiguous-reference, missing-owner, ambiguous-date, ambiguous-time, missing-timezone, ambiguous-acceptance, possible-conflict only as applicable. Do not create general uncertainty about every unknown detail. A possible-conflict flag references exactly two potentially incompatible preference claims with the same identified holder and scoped subject. Unknown subject in an operational question is material; an unknown author of an ordinary object-capacity fact need not trigger a flag. All useful claims require routine manual review regardless of flags. Do not emit a proposal merely to explain why irrelevant text was skipped.
